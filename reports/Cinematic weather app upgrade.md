# Build Rosa a sky worth refracting

Rosa can reach a commercial, cinematic standard without changing engines. Its AGSL-in-Compose pipeline is the right foundation, because it is what lets every Liquid Glass surface refract the live sky and take its light from it, and the app already has details that market leaders treat as signatures: a lens-like rain, frost and mist window pane, glass lit from the real sun and moon, envelope haptics, and real sun and moon positions. What looks flat is the part users see most. The sky is a two-colour vertical gradient under two 2D noise cloud decks, and the sun is held in a box of about 80 dp beside the numerals, drawn as the same white disc at 17:40 as at 09:13, with no horizon. Rain and a downpour are hard to tell apart, hail is never drawn, and weather changes play as 1.4-second blends, or as single-frame cuts while scrubbing. The fix is art direction more than GPU power: a virtual camera, a lookup-table atmosphere of the kind Unreal Engine ships (about 1 ms for a whole sky on an iPhone 6s), a horizon taken from the app's own calendar painter, lit layered clouds, a distinct look for each condition, staged transitions, time-lapse scrubbing, and opt-in sound paired with the existing haptics. Most of it lowers GPU work per second, because it moves expensive noise into cached layers and tables built on the CPU. Two things must come first. One is a measurement-and-safety phase, since the app has never run on a physical device or emulator, animates its sky at a fixed 30 fps and never lets Auto choose Cinematic. The other is a clean-room rewrite of the pane's drop field, which the source code credits to a Shadertoy published under a non-commercial licence. The closing plan orders the work into six phases across four quality tiers (Still, Battery, Balanced, Cinematic). It holds the sky pipeline to a quarter of each vsync period and to today's energy use at each tier, sets comfort rules for motion, flashing and sound, and pairs every step with a JVM-side check and an on-device gate.

*Code paths are relative to the repository at HEAD `f9392f8` (v2.6.1), with line numbers at that commit. `DS/` is `core/designsystem/src/main/kotlin/app/rosa/weather/core/designsystem/`, `MODEL/` is `core/model/src/main/kotlin/app/rosa/weather/core/model/`, `APP/` is `app/src/main/kotlin/app/rosa/weather/`, `WID/` is `widget/src/main/kotlin/app/rosa/weather/widget/`, and `DATA/` is `core/data/src/main/kotlin/app/rosa/weather/core/data/`.*

## Rosa already has the details that leading apps treat as signatures

Apple set today's bar in 2021, when its Weather app gained real-time backgrounds in "thousands of variations" keyed to sun position, clouds and rain ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/06/07/apple-finally-updates-its-weather-app-with-dynamic-backgrounds-maps-and-way-more-data/)), **limited to A12-class chips or newer** ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/guide/ios-15-weather-app/)). Its most-copied touch is physical: rain and snow land on the tops of the forecast cards, a detail that a course rebuilding the app gives its own episode on "particle interaction with UI elements" ([Hacking with Swift+](https://www.hackingwithswift.com/plus/remaking-weather); [MacRumors Forums](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/apples-attention-to-detail-the-weather-app.2299694/)). Coverage of iOS 16 through iOS 27 describes new surfaces, Liquid Glass controls and a Highlights section rather than a rebuilt sky ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/guide/ios-27-weather/)), so the benchmark is five years old. Google chose restraint. Pixel Weather launched with condition-tinted gradients and AI-drawn skylines for 300+ cities, kept "subtle in the background" ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2024/10/31/pixel-weather-froggy-design/)). It then added sun glints, drifting clouds and raindrops whose **vibrations land with the drops** ([Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/google-pixel-weather-hidden-play-pause-button-animations/)). The animations and vibrations need a Pixel 8 or newer and **stop in Battery Saver**, and the vibrations also switch off automatically in dangerous weather ([Google Pixel Help](https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/15266029?hl=en)).

Third-party apps fill the gaps the platform apps leave. In (Not Boring) Weather, the rain particle count follows real intensity, clouds are generated from real coverage, and the forecast bar scrubs "like a timelapse shot" ([!Boring](https://notbor.ing/product/weather)); it also has original audio for every weather event ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/story/id1556321408)). Yahoo Weather, a 2013 Apple Design Award winner ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/221206/wwdc-2013-the-apple-design-award-winners.html)), showed the cheapest premium trick: windmills turning at the real wind speed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/04/18/yahoos-surprisingly-gorgeous-new-ios-weather-app-centers-around-crowdsourced-photos)). The Weather Channel's Emmy-winning mixed-reality storm surge showed the other extreme. It kept photoreal drama for serious weather and tied it to an action such as evacuating ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/hurricane-milton-storm-surge-simulation-unnreal-florida-1851668336); [Television Academy](https://www.televisionacademy.com/features/news/mix/immersed-story)).

Taken together, the leaders share a few compositing rules rather than an art style. Rosa is far ahead on some of those rules and well behind on others.

Rosa built things in the reverse of the usual order. It perfected the lens and the glass, which leaders treat as finishing touches, and left the sky, which they treat as the foundation, at placeholder quality. That gives the upgrade unusual leverage. Each glass pane computes its own light toward the sun or moon position the sky publishes, and flashes with the sky's lightning, so every improvement to the sky also re-lights the interface, from golden rims at sunset to bolt-lit edges in a storm. The plan therefore keeps the pane, the `GlassEnvironment` coupling, the single shared backdrop with its baked frost, the interpolable `SkyParams` schema, the spring and haptic vocabulary, and the real sun and moon positions, and rebuilds what sits behind them.

The rebuild should stay inside AGSL and Compose, because each strong alternative breaks something. Filament brings physically based rendering, HDR bloom and filmic tone mapping ([Filament](https://github.com/google/filament)), and bundled time-lapse video can look photoreal, as Forza Horizon's captured skies do ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024091/Shoot-for-the-Sky-The)). Both, however, render into a `SurfaceView`, which the app's own UI renderer cannot sample, so Liquid Glass would lose the live refraction that defines Rosa. A `TextureView` can be sampled, but for video Media3 measures it at up to 30% more power than a `SurfaceView` ([Media3](https://developer.android.com/media/media3/exoplayer/battery-consumption)), and video cannot react to live wind or sun angle anyway. The platform's own answer is AGSL. AOSP's `weathereffects` module, whose shaders line up one-to-one with Pixel's Rain, Snow, Fog and Sun wallpaper effects, ships 18 AGSL files, and the headers checked carry the Apache 2.0 licence. They include rain on glass, layered rain with splashes around an outline buffer, snow accumulation, god rays with lens flare, and colour grading with a lookup table ([AOSP](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/libs/systemui/+/refs/heads/main/weathereffects/graphics/assets/shaders/)). Nor should Rosa chase Android 17 QPR2's native `setBackdropRenderEffect`: in Chris Banes's benchmark it had higher CPU frame times than source-based capture when the content behind was changing ([chrisbanes.me](https://chrisbanes.me/posts/compose-progressive-blur/)), and changing content is Rosa's permanent case. The job is to restructure the pipeline, not replace it.

## A camera, an atmosphere and a horizon fix the flat sky

The sky shader starts from `mix(zenith, horizon, pow(h, 1.25))`, so colour varies with screen height only (`DS/sky/SkyShaders.kt` L107). It then adds a radial glow around the sun or moon and a band along the bottom edge (L114–115). The sun or moon follows its real path, but only inside a box spanning 73–90% of the scene's width and 17–25% of its height, which Home shrinks to ±40 dp around the weather-icon slot beside the numerals (`DS/sky/SkyStage.kt`; `APP/ui/home/HomeScreen.kt` L641–685).

The Robolectric test renders of the home screen at 09:13 and 17:40 show the result (`app/build/screens/home-morning.png`, `home-evening.png`). The evening's warmth is a peach band along the bottom edge, while the sun is the same white disc in the same upper-right slot as in the morning. The glow and the low sun sit half a screen apart, and the sun never meets a horizon. Other details add to the flatness. Of the palette's nine sun-elevation keys, only the 0° and 4° keys have warm horizon colours, and the sky is blue again by 10° (`MODEL/SkyPalette.kt` L63–74). The model computes a `DayPhase.GoldenHour` that the renderer never reads (`MODEL/Astronomy.kt` L79). Cloud silver linings are gated by `sunUp` (`DS/sky/SkyShaders.kt` L159), so clouds never catch moonlight. Only one body is drawn at a time, so a daytime moon never appears, and the sky pops from sun to moon halfway through any blend (`DS/sky/SkyScene.kt` L100). The moon's lit side is fixed for the northern hemisphere.

### A virtual camera turns the stage box into a framing rule

The enabling change is a virtual camera (yaw, pitch and field of view) in place of the sun's box, because nearly everything that makes a sky look cinematic is defined by angles. A primary rainbow sits about 42° from the point opposite the sun, with its secondary bow at 50–53° ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow)). A 22° halo rings the sun or moon behind thin cirrostratus cloud ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22%C2%B0_halo)). The Belt of Venus glows 10–20° above the horizon opposite the sun, over Earth's shadow ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belt_of_Venus)). God rays radiate from the light's position on screen ([GPU Gems 3](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-ii-light-and-shadows/chapter-13-volumetric-light-scattering-post-process)), and a lookup-table (LUT) sky is indexed by view direction ([Hillaire 2020](https://sebh.github.io/publications/egsr2020.pdf)). None of these can be placed inside a fixed box on the screen.

With a camera, the README's promise that the sun never goes behind the digits becomes a framing rule rather than a cage. The camera picks yaw and pitch so the real sun lands in free sky or in the weather-icon slot. That keeps the existing hand-off in which the icon steps aside for the real sun (`APP/ui/home/HomeScreen.kt` L458–464). The camera lets a setting sun rest on the horizon, turns away from the sun after showers so a rainbow can appear, and uses a longer virtual lens at sunrise and moonrise, the way a telephoto shot enlarges a setting disc. When the sun leaves the frame, the sky should publish the light's direction instead of a screen position, so the glass keeps its lighting. The fixed moods (Light, Evening, Dark) become virtual sun elevations fed to the same model, so they keep coherent light on the glass instead of falling back to the default resting light.

### A 96×50 lookup table buys a physically lit atmosphere

The atmosphere should follow Sébastien Hillaire's production technique, which runs on everything from an iPhone 6s to consoles and ships in Unreal Engine ([Hillaire 2020](https://sebh.github.io/publications/egsr2020.pdf)). It rests on three small lookup tables. The transmittance table (256×64) and the multiple-scattering table (32×32) depend only on atmosphere constants, so Rosa can precompute them offline in a few aerosol variants. The sky-view table is 96×50 texels with 8 steps on mobile, and it cost 0.27 ms on an iPhone 6s. **The whole Fortnite sky cost about 1 ms on that phone**, and the lower table quality was "not noticeable to the naked eye". The atmosphere changes only when the sun moves or the weather changes. Rosa can therefore rebuild the 4,800-texel sky-view table on a background thread when either happens, and pass it to the shader as a `BitmapShader` input. That leaves one filtered texture read plus an analytic sun per pixel. The input must set `FILTER_MODE_LINEAR` explicitly, because a `BitmapShader` feeding a `RuntimeShader` defaults to nearest-neighbour filtering ([AOSP](https://github.com/aosp-mirror/platform_frameworks_base/blob/master/graphics/java/android/graphics/BitmapShader.java)).

The model has hooks for both physics and art direction. Hillaire models ozone as a pure absorber because it is "key to achieving sky-blue colors when the sun is at the horizon". Ozone's Chappuis absorption band is what makes the blue hour blue ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chappuis_absorption)). Hillaire's own sunset example exaggerates Rayleigh scattering fivefold as art direction. Aerosol density is the natural input for haze. Rosa already fetches `visibility`, and Open-Meteo's Air Quality API offers `aerosol_optical_depth` and Saharan `dust` worldwide ([Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/air-quality-api)). `SkyPalette` then stops being the source of the gradient and becomes a colour grade on top. Each `DayPhase` gets its own lift, gamma, gain and split-toning, which keep the palette's "filmic, slightly warm" identity while the physically derived light does the heavy lifting.

The sun disc is drawn after the table lookup, as Hillaire does, with a soft glow from a Cornette–Shanks or Henyey–Greenstein scattering term (Hillaire's default g is 0.8). On the top tier, three to six lens-flare ghosts can be computed along the line from the sun to the screen centre. Because the sun's position is known, this avoids the downsample, threshold and blur passes of Chapman's image-space flare ([Chapman](https://john-chapman.github.io/2017/11/05/pseudo-lens-flare.html)). No Android timings for the technique were found, and the iPhone 6s figures are the closest evidence, so the Phase 0 measurement harness must confirm them.

### The calendar painter already holds the missing horizon

A horizon gives the light somewhere to land, and Rosa already has the tools to draw one. The calendar widget's `Painting.kt` builds matte-painted landscapes: ridges in aerial perspective, water with mirror, ripple and glint, fog, light rays, a rainbow and a Milky Way (`WID/render/calendar/Painting.kt` L79–675). It is part of a calendar renderer of roughly 9,100 lines. A horizon strip extracted from it (ridge, sea or roofline silhouettes, chosen per place) can live in a cached bitmap. It would be redrawn only when the time of day or the condition changes, and tinted by the atmosphere's aerial perspective. That one strip lets the sunset glow and the setting sun share a line, gives fog something to hug, snow something to settle on and heavy rain a surface to splash on. It also supplies the "subject" that AOSP's rain stack draws between far and near rain. Kept low and mostly behind the cards, it follows Pixel's lesson that a sense of place reads as premium when it stays "subtle in the background" ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2024/10/31/pixel-weather-froggy-design/)).

### Clouds need light and layers, not volume

Guerrilla's ray-marched clouds for Horizon Zero Dawn fit into about 2 ms on a PS4, but only after rendering at quarter resolution, updating one pixel in sixteen each frame and reusing the previous frame for the rest. Before that, the same shader cost around 20 ms ([Guerrilla](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/the-real-time-volumetric-cloudscapes-of-horizon-zero-dawn)). AGSL gives a shader no access to the previous frame, so that trick is unavailable, and a phone has less to spare. An Adreno 619, found in mid-range Snapdragon chips, peaks at 83–115 GFLOPS ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adreno)). By my estimate, giving the background a quarter of that at 1080×2400 and 60 Hz leaves about 160 FLOPs per pixel. That is roughly the price of a single five-octave noise stack, so per-pixel cloud noise at full resolution and full rate does not fit.

The workable recipe is layered 2D clouds whose lighting fakes depth. It combines four ingredients. The first is Inigo Quilez's dynamic fBm noise, whose octaves drift at different speeds and which is shaded by sampling density toward the sun ([Inigo Quilez](https://iquilezles.org/articles/dynclouds/)). The second is Horizon's "Beer–Powder" darkening and its Henyey–Greenstein silver lining. The third is curl-noise distortion for wisps ([Bridson et al.](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~rbridson/docs/bridson-siggraph2007-curlnoise.pdf)). The fourth is two to four decks mapped to Open-Meteo's `cloud_cover_low`, `cloud_cover_mid` and `cloud_cover_high` ([Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs)), which Rosa does not yet request (`DATA/network/OpenMeteoClient.kt` L76–85). The decks should drift along `wind_direction_10m`, which Rosa fetches but never uses. Removing the `sunUp` gate lets moonlight light their edges too. Rendered at a quarter to a sixteenth of the pixels, and refreshed at 15–30 Hz as slow drift allows, they cost a fraction of today's full-rate noise.

Night and the sun and moon deserve the same care. Each star today is one hashed candidate per 26-pixel cell, rendered at the sky's reduced scale and then upscaled. Stars belong in the full-resolution pass as two or three layers. Their colour and brightness should come from a hashed temperature and magnitude, the inputs of Jensen's physically based night-sky model ([Jensen et al.](http://graphics.stanford.edu/~henrik/papers/nightsky/)), and they should twinkle most near the horizon. Above them can sit the calendar painter's Milky Way band, and occasional meteors triggered from the CPU, which the calendar's live tiles already animate (`WID/motion/LiveWeather.kt` L45–100). The moon should become a sphere lit from the sun's real direction, which fixes the southern-hemisphere mirror for free. It should show earthshine on the dark side of thin crescents: sunlight reflected by Earth, visible a few days either side of new moon ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetshine)). Independent weights for sun and moon let a daytime moon hang in a blue sky, and let blends crossfade instead of popping.

### Three clocks and a last-pass grade keep it affordable

All of this implies a frame with three update rates. The code already uses this pattern for the pane, which it renders once per sky frame and caches (`DS/sky/SkyScene.kt` L365–370).

| Clock | Layers | When they redraw |
|---|---|---|
| Cached | Atmosphere table, horizon, Milky Way | Only when they change |
| Slow | Cloud decks, fog banks | Offscreen at low resolution, 10–30 Hz |
| Fast | Rain and snow streaks, the pane, stars, dither | At pane resolution, every frame in which they move |

Post-processing moves into the last pass, because separate full-screen passes are limited by memory bandwidth on mobile GPUs. In one Arm case study, bloom alone cost 3 ms, while texture-based alternatives cost under 1 ms ([Arm](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/post-processing-effects-on-mobile-optimization-and-alternatives)). The bright sources are known, so their glow can be computed directly instead of blurred. The tone curve can be Narkowicz's ACES fit for a filmic look, or Khronos PBR Neutral where interface colours must stay true ([64.github.io](https://64.github.io/tonemapping/); [Khronos](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/ToneMapping/blob/main/PBR_Neutral/README.md)). A vignette costs two operations. Dual-filter bloom, which needs about 7% of the bandwidth of a linear-sampling blur ([Bjørge](https://community.arm.com/cfs-file/__key/communityserver-blogs-components-weblogfiles/00-00-00-20-66/siggraph2015_2D00_mmg_2D00_marius_2D00_notes.pdf)), stays optional on the top tier.

Dithering needs a specific fix. Today's grain is uniform noise of ±0.009, about ±2.3 levels at 8 bits. It is reseeded every frame inside the reduced-resolution sky layer, which the upscale then smooths (`DS/sky/SkyShaders.kt` L216–217). Playdead's Mikkel Gjøl recommends dither of about one level with a triangular distribution, applied per channel just before 8-bit output and changed every frame ([Gjøl](https://loopit.dk/banding_in_games.pdf)). Rosa can follow that while the sky is animating anyway, and freeze the pattern when the sky is idle, so identical frames need not be redrawn.

### HDR is the last ten per cent, not the first

A window opts into HDR with `COLOR_MODE_HDR` from API 34, and Android 15 adds `setDesiredHdrHeadroom`. Google advises about 1.5–2× headroom for screens that are mostly SDR. It warns that HDR highlights make nearby SDR text look greyer, and tells apps not to brighten SDR content to compensate ([Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2025/09/hdr-and-user-interfaces.html)). Only Ultra HDR images are documented to use that headroom ([Android](https://developer.android.com/media/grow/ultra-hdr/display)). Whether AGSL output above 1.0 reaches the panel is unverified, and one developer reports ColorOS devices whose HDR/SDR ratio is stuck at 1.0 ([fliks PR](https://github.com/fliks-app/fliks/pull/1247)). Compose 1.12 announces wide-gamut and HDR rendering across its pipeline ([Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/08/jetpack-compose-august-2026-release.html)). Rosa's Compose BOM (`2026.09.00`) is newer than the August 2026 BOM that shipped it, so a prototype is cheap. HDR sun glare and lightning belong at the end of the plan, as a prototype for the Cinematic tier that switches on only while a bright highlight is on screen.

## Each of the 21 conditions needs its own look, not a density setting

`WeatherVisual.from` maps Rosa's 21 conditions onto a handful of fixed values (`MODEL/WeatherCondition.kt` L95–117), and many conditions differ in only one or two of them. I measured the shader gallery's own renders, taking the mean CIE76 colour difference between downscaled frames in `core/designsystem/build/weather/`.

| Pair | Mean colour difference |
|---|---|
| Rain vs downpour | **3.1 ΔE** |
| Snow vs frost | **0.3 ΔE** |
| Rain vs snow (plainly different) | 16.2 ΔE |

The snow–frost figure is partly a quirk of the test data, because at −7 °C the snow scene's pane is already frosted. That still makes the point that falling snow barely registers. Fog renders as a uniform milky haze with a visible sun disc. The storm bolt is a soft smear with one short fork, because it is drawn at the sky's reduced resolution and then blurred by the pane (`DS/sky/SkyShaders.kt` L195–214).

Some weather cannot appear at all. `ThunderstormHail` carries a hail value of 0.7, but `SkyParams` has no field for it, so hail is never drawn in the app (`DS/sky/SkyScene.kt` L67–94). Freezing rain and sleet are rain plus a few flakes. Wind is a speed without a direction, although direction and gusts are fetched. Haze, dust and smoke are impossible, because Open-Meteo sends no such weather codes and Rosa requests no aerosol data ([Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs)). The test data explains why these gaps went unnoticed. `SampleForecast` has six scenarios and the shader gallery six scenes, and none of them is hail, sleet, freezing rain, dry wind or golden hour. The gallery also writes PNGs without comparing them to a reference (`WeatherShaderGalleryTest.kt` in `core/designsystem/src/test/`).

### Rain intensity should change the picture, not only the density

The production references agree on the ingredients. ATI's ToyShop demo rendered many layers of rain in one full-screen pass with a single texture read. It used one shared direction vector for every drop, biased drops toward white the way film crews add milk to rain water, and made drops more transparent during lightning ([Tatarchuk 2006](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2006/Chapter3-Artist-Directable_Real-Time_Rain_Rendering_in_City_Environments.pdf)). Sébastien Lagarde's rain added four animated ripple layers, wet darkening and puddles, for about 2.8 ms on a PS3 at 720p ([Lagarde](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2013/01/03/water-drop-2b-dynamic-rain-and-its-effects/)).

For Rosa this becomes a ladder. Drizzle is one or two thin, far layers inside a mist veil, with static drops on the pane. Rain is three layers, with splashes along the horizon strip. Heavy rain is five layers, with low-frequency "curtains" sweeping across, a fully fogged pane cut by trails, and darker, lower cloud. Streak length should equal fall speed times a virtual camera shutter, which builds motion blur into each streak. This matters because today the nearest layer falls 2.4 screen heights a second at 30 updates a second, about 190 pixels between frames on a 2,340-pixel screen, which strobes on a 120 Hz panel.

### Cards become window frames that weather can touch

Weather should touch the interface in a way that fits Rosa's window metaphor. Apple lets rain hit the tops of its cards. In Rosa, though, the rain falls outside a window pane that the cards float in front of, so rain striking a card would break the depth. The consistent version, a design proposal rather than an observed pattern, treats the cards as frames set into the glass. Sliding drops stop and pool along a card's top edge and drip from its corners. Snow builds a cap along top edges over the minutes it falls, and freezing rain glazes the edges with icy glints. These edge effects belong in each card's own glass shader, beside the frost veins it already grows from the edge (`DS/glass/GlassShaders.kt`). They then stay locked to the card through a 120 Hz scroll, even though the pane redraws at 30 Hz. The pane only needs the card rectangles, passed as a small fixed-size array, to stop drops at those edges. Two Apache-licensed AOSP shaders are close technical references. `snow_accumulation.agsl` builds snow up along the vertical edges of a foreground mask ([AOSP](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/libs/systemui/+/refs/heads/main/weathereffects/graphics/assets/shaders/snow_accumulation.agsl)), and `rain_shower_layer.agsl` splashes rain around an outline ([AOSP](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/libs/systemui/+/refs/heads/main/weathereffects/graphics/assets/shaders/rain_shower_layer.agsl)).

### Hail, snow, fog and haze each get a signature

Hail and sleet suit CPU particles, because there are few of them and they bounce. Particles computed in the shader on a hashed grid cost the same per pixel however many there are, which suits dense rain and snow. Canvas particles cost per particle, which suits a few dozen pellets that bounce and spin off the horizon strip and the card tops, flash briefly on impact and trigger a sparse low haptic tick. Sleet mixes short, bright pellets into the rain layers. No benchmarked app has a verified hail or sleet treatment, so this is open ground for a signature effect.

Snow should read as depth and weather rather than specks. Near flakes are large, soft and translucent, like out-of-focus bokeh, while far flakes are small and sharp. Flakes drift sideways on a curl-noise field broken by occasional gusts, and heavy snow brings whiteout sweeps. This is the multi-layer, depth-of-field approach of the widely ported "Just snow" shader ([libretro](https://github.com/libretro/glsl-shaders/blob/master/borders/resources/snow.glsl)). Frost then separates from snow through art direction: frost is a clear, cold sky with the frame icing over, and snow is milky light with falling flakes.

Fog gets Inigo Quilez's analytic height fog. Its sun-aware colour gives "glowing/blooming and other light scattering effects without actually doing any multipass", for about one extra division per pixel ([Inigo Quilez](https://iquilezles.org/articles/fog/)). Add one or two low-resolution fog banks drifting along the horizon, and god rays through gaps at golden hour. Rime fog adds frost crystals on the window frame. Haze and dust reuse the atmosphere's aerosol term, with warm colour absorption and a reddened sun.

### Photosensitivity limits the storm's flashes, not its drama

Storms are where the current renderer is weakest and where drama is cheapest to add. Each bolt should be generated on the CPU per strike by midpoint displacement, halving the offset each generation and occasionally splitting a branch ([Drilian](https://www.drilian.com/posts/2009.02.25-lightning-bolts/)). It should be drawn after the pane is rendered, as a crisp near-white core with bluish glow strokes, so the pane's blur no longer smears it. Most of a storm's impact comes from the flash lighting everything else. ToyShop passed lightning brightness to every rain shader ([Tatarchuk 2006](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2006/Chapter3-Artist-Directable_Real-Time_Rain_Rendering_in_City_Environments.pdf)), and Guerrilla added "a near zero cost method" for lighting inside clouds ([Guerrilla](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/nubis-evolved)). In 2D, that becomes a radial light at a random point inside the cloud deck, masked by cloud density, plus a brief lift in fog and streak brightness. Publishing the bolt's position through `GlassEnvironment`, next to the flash value it already carries, makes the card edges facing the strike flare hardest. The strike rate could follow Open-Meteo's 15-minute `lightning_potential` where weather models provide it ([Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs)), instead of a fixed random interval of 3.5–11 s.

The hard limit is photosensitivity. WCAG 2.3.1 allows no more than three flashes in any one second, and a full-screen flash on a phone far exceeds its area threshold, so that frequency limit applies directly ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html)). Today's lightning is two full-screen pulses within about 120 ms, with at least 3.5 s between strikes (`DS/sky/SkyScene.kt` L214–233), which complies. The flicker of real lightning strokes must therefore stay inside the bolt's small area, or below a 10% change in luminance. It must never become extra full-screen pulses.

A single wind vector (speed, direction and gust events) should drive cloud drift, rain slant (which today always leans the same way), snow drift, fog movement and a gentle sway in the horizon silhouette. Yahoo's windmills showed how much a detail driven by real data reads as craft. The camera also makes rare optical effects nearly free. A rainbow sits about 42° from the point opposite the sun, with its faint reversed secondary bow and the darker Alexander band between them. It should appear only after showers, when the sun is behind the camera and below 42° ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow)). A 22° halo, red on its inner edge, belongs where high thin cloud veils the sun or moon. An aurora can be drawn cheaply as a 2D curtain shape multiplied by a height profile ([Lawlor and Genetti](https://www.cs.uaf.edu/~olawlor/2011/aurora_WSCG_2011.pdf)), green low and red above ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora)). It needs a geomagnetic data source that this research did not identify, so it stays optional.

| Condition | Rosa today | Signature (Cinematic, Balanced) | Battery; Still |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drizzle | Sparse streaks, small drops | Thin far layers in a mist veil, static drops, no splashes | 1 layer; frozen drops |
| Rain to heavy rain | Same renderer, only density changes | 3 to 5 layers with streak blur, curtains, horizon splashes, drops pooling on card tops, fully fogged pane | 2 layers; frozen frame |
| Freezing rain, sleet | Rain plus a few flakes | Pellets among the streaks, icy glints and glaze on glass edges | Rain preset; glazed still |

### The pane's licence must be cleared before release

One effect needs rework before any of this ships commercially. The source code credits the pane's drop field as "after Martijn Steinrucken's 'Heartfelt'" (`DS/sky/SkyShaders.kt` L327). Heartfelt's header licenses it under Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 3.0 ([Shadertoy](https://www.shadertoy.com/view/ltffzl); [source copy](https://github.com/sanxincao/shadertoy/blob/master/heartfelt.glsl)). Rosa's licence files and its in-app licences text cover only its two typefaces and Open-Meteo. Whether Rosa's version counts as a derivative work is a legal question. The engineering answer is a clean-room re-derivation from the published technique of hashed grid cells, a sawtooth slide with a sideways wiggle, trails and a focus mask. AOSP's Apache-licensed `glass_rain.agsl` is a permissive reference ([AOSP](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/libs/systemui/+/refs/heads/main/weathereffects/graphics/assets/shaders/glass_rain.agsl)). The rewrite is also a performance win. AGSL documents no derivative functions ([AGSL quick reference](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/graphics/agsl/agsl-quick-reference)), so Rosa evaluates the drop field three times per pixel to get its surface normals, which Heartfelt's own comments call about three times costlier than using derivatives. Because the drops are circles, their gradients can be computed exactly, which removes the two extra evaluations.

### Widgets can share the look within RemoteViews limits

`RemoteViews` accepts no custom views ([Android](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/appwidgets)), so widgets cannot run shaders between updates. Rosa's widgets already push that limit with animated-vector rain, snow and storm tiles (`WID/motion/LiveWeather.kt` L19–180). The gap is that widget skies come from a second renderer, drawn on the CPU, with blob clouds and a thin-line bolt (`WID/render/WidgetBackground.kt` L350–521). Rendering widget backgrounds from the rebuilt sky pipeline would give one look everywhere. The research found no documented way to render AGSL offscreen in a background worker, though, so that step needs a prototype. New tiles for drifting cloud, fog, twinkling stars and meteors (the calendar already animates the last two), plus hail, sleet and wind, would complete the set. Android 15's generated widget previews can show the real current sky in the widget picker ([Android 15](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/15/features)).

## Choreography, touch and sound turn weather changes into events

Every change of state in Rosa is a blend of sky parameters. Weather and time changes restart a 1,400 ms animation from wherever the last one had reached (`DS/sky/SkyScene.kt` L197–212), so an update that arrives mid-blend restarts from zero velocity, and the sun or moon flips at the halfway mark. City swipes blend the sky by pager position, so a jump across several cities made in code probably flashes through every sky in between (`APP/ui/home/HomeScreen.kt` L195–222). Timeline scrubbing switches to immediate mode while the weather code steps at each hour, so rain, snow and lightning appear or vanish in a single frame (L241–249; `MODEL/ForecastMoment.kt` L94–107). Every cold start plays an accidental 1.4 s morph from a sunny sample sky over Moscow to the local sky (`APP/ui/common/SkyController.kt` L90–92). The sky has no camera, and it ignores scrolling and pull-to-refresh. Its advertised tap ripple and finger wipe are probably unreachable on Home. The sky's touch handler (`pointerInput`) sits beneath the full-screen pager and list inside `SkyBackdrop`'s box, and Compose stops passing a touch to lower siblings once one has handled it (`DS/component/SkyBackdrop.kt` L113–131). That last point is inferred from the code, not reproduced.

### Springs and staging replace the 1.4-second blend

A small director should stage each weather change as an event. Arriving rain starts with cloud rolling in from upwind, light dimming and a few drops striking the pane, and only then do the streaks thicken. Clearing reverses it: drops keep sliding after the rain stops, and light breaks through in rays. The motion should use springs rather than fixed-duration animations, because "a spring animation uses the velocity it had when it was retargeted" ([WWDC23](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10158/)). That removes the jolt when a new update arrives mid-blend. Sky colour and opacity need springs that settle without overshoot. Material 3 Expressive calls these "effects" springs and reserves them for properties "where there shouldn't be any overshoot" ([Material 3](https://m3.material.io/blog/m3-expressive-motion-theming)), because a sky that overshoots its colour looks like a glitch. Apple's conversion, stiffness = (2π ÷ duration)², gives a stiffness of about 9.9 for a two-second perceived duration and 4.4 for three seconds. Rosa's existing "gel" spring (damping 0.7, stiffness 158, about 0.5 s with a bounce of 0.3) sits within Apple's advice to be cautious above a bounce of about 0.4. It should remain the spring for gestures (`DS/motion/RosaMotion.kt` L13–30).

### Scrubbing, city changes and launch become directed moves

Scrubbing should play as a time-lapse, the pattern (Not Boring) sells as moving through a day "like a timelapse shot" ([!Boring](https://notbor.ing/product/weather)). That needs weather visuals that interpolate between hours instead of stepping with the nearest weather code. It also needs cloud drift sped up 20- to 60-fold while the finger moves, the sun sweeping its real arc through the camera, and exposure easing as the light changes. The hourly timeline's magnifier already ticks each hour and marks sunrise, sunset and midnight with haptics (`APP/ui/home/HourlyTimeline.kt` L76–178, L304–312). During a scrub, the slow cloud layer must briefly refresh at the full frame rate, which the budget allows while the user is touching the screen.

City changes treat the sky as a persistent layer. A swipe adds a slight camera pan in the swipe's direction, like the parallax between cities that Yahoo was praised for in 2013 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/04/18/yahoos-surprisingly-gorgeous-new-ios-weather-app-centers-around-crowdsourced-photos)). A jump made in code blends straight from the first sky to the last. Launch should start from the selected city's cached forecast at the real time, never from a sample city. The atmosphere needs only the clock and coordinates, so the sky can render before any network data arrives and double as the loading state, under a static, layout-matching skeleton. NN/g cautions that animated skeletons can create accessibility problems ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens/)). When fresh data lands, the conditions fade in on a no-overshoot spring. A `RuntimeShader` compiles its source when it is constructed ([HotSwan](https://hotswan.dev/blog/compose-agsl-shader-tuning)), and Google's Baseline Profile documentation covers code execution, not GPU shader compilation ([Baseline Profiles](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/baselineprofiles/overview)). Each sky shader should therefore be built and drawn once during the 900 ms splash.

### Touch, tilt and scroll give the sky a camera operator

Fixing touch reachability means watching touches at the backdrop's parent without consuming them. A tap anywhere not claimed by a control then starts the ripple, and moving the wipe to a long-press drag stops it fighting the scroll. Pull-to-refresh should end with its glass drop falling into the pane as a ripple, and a completed refresh can sweep a soft band of light across the sky. A long press on the large weather icon can start the ambient sound, echoing the rain soundscape Pixel hides behind its condition icon ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2024/10/31/pixel-weather-froggy-design/)).

Tilt parallax should move from the gravity sensor at `SENSOR_DELAY_UI` (`DS/sensor/Tilt.kt` L51–61) to `TYPE_GAME_ROTATION_VECTOR`. That sensor fuses gyroscope and accelerometer and must not use the magnetometer, so it avoids sudden magnetic jumps ([AOSP](https://source.android.com/docs/core/interaction/sensors/sensor-types)). It should sample at `SENSOR_DELAY_GAME` (every 20 ms) on the top tier only, and unregister whenever the sky is not visible ([Android](https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/sensors/sensors_overview)). Parallax should vary with depth, so the sky at infinity barely moves and near rain moves most. The scene should render 1–2% oversize, so tilting never reveals an edge.

Scrolling becomes a camera move too. As detail cards rise, the camera tilts toward the horizon, and the sky dims and softens behind the text, as Yahoo blurred its photo when the user pulled up ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/04/18/yahoos-surprisingly-gorgeous-new-ios-weather-app-centers-around-crowdsourced-photos)). That also helps legibility, which is already disputed for Liquid Glass ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/guide/ios-26-liquid-glass/)).

### Sound stays opt-in because half of phones are on vibrate

Sound is the largest missing channel and the most intrusive one, and Rosa has no audio code at all. (Not Boring)'s designers recommend eight to twelve variations per sound, so repetition becomes texture. They also recommend almost always pairing sound with haptics, and giving users fine-grained control ([!Boring](https://notbor.ing/words/the-sound-of-software)). Reach is limited by habit. In a self-selected Android Authority poll, 46% of respondents kept their phones on vibrate or silent ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/sound-profile-vibrate-ring-silent-poll-results-1224669/)), and about half the participants in a small Microsoft Research field study did the same ([Chang et al.](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/p6-chang.pdf)). Ambient sound should therefore be opt-in. Media3 ExoPlayer can loop 30–90 s recordings for drizzle, rain, downpour, wind and night, crossfaded by live intensity and wind speed. Interface sounds should play through `SoundPool` under the sonification audio usage, muted in vibrate or silent mode. Android's audio focus rules decide the rest ([Android](https://developer.android.com/media/optimize/audio-focus)). Since Android 15, an app that is not in the foreground cannot get audio focus, and since Android 12 the system fades out media players when another app takes focus. So ambient sound must never start over the user's music, and it must stop in the background without a foreground service.

Thunder is the key moment, and Rosa is closer to it than it looks. The flash already schedules an envelope rumble 250–2,450 ms later, depending on the strike's distance (`DS/sky/SkyScene.kt` L214–233; `DS/haptics/RosaHaptics.kt` L136). Adding audio means driving the haptic from the audio clock rather than from the flash. In a driving study, about one participant in five noticed audio–haptic offsets of 50–65 ms ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.05451)), so the target is under 50 ms. On devices that support it, Android 12's `HapticGenerator` derives haptics directly from the audio ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/android-12-audio-coupled-haptic-effect/)). Everything else stays sparse. Android's guidance is that buzzy feedback is worse than none, and that the most frequent events deserve the subtlest feedback ([Android](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/haptics/haptics-principles)). A rain texture should play only while a finger is on the rain, as sparse low ticks, never as continuous ambient vibration. Following Pixel, playful haptics switch off in dangerous weather and in Battery Saver.

## The plan: six phases, four tiers, one frame-budget rule

The order follows risk, not glamour. Phase 0 comes first for three reasons. The README states that the app has never run on a physical device or emulator, because the development environment lacks hardware virtualisation, so every budget in this report is a hypothesis until measured. Each later phase adds GPU work that needs a governor and a frame budget to fit inside. And the licence and photosensitivity items block release. After Phase 0, rendering (Phases 1–2), choreography (Phase 3) and audio (Phase 4) can run as parallel tracks. Verification then runs at three levels. On the JVM, Robolectric already renders AGSL in this environment, so reference-image tests, distinctness checks, flash tests, governor logic and touch reachability can all run in CI. On devices, Macrobenchmark and Perfetto measure frames and energy, on a physical phone or a cloud device lab covering at least a mid-range Adreno, a Mali GPU and a Pixel 6 or newer. In the field, JankStats tags every frame with its scene and tier.

### Four tiers, moved by a live governor

Today's `SceneQuality` tiers are sound but static. `rememberSceneQuality` reads Battery Saver and thermal status once, inside `remember`. Auto never offers Cinematic, and a device that struggles is downgraded for the rest of the session (`DS/component/SkyBackdrop.kt` L133–151; `DS/motion/FramePacing.kt`). Rosa should add a `Still` tier and a governor that moves between tiers. The governor listens for thermal status changes and for Battery Saver broadcasts registered at runtime. It polls `getThermalHeadroom` no more than once every 10 s, as Android's Adaptive Performance (ADPF) documentation requires ([ADPF](https://developer.android.com/games/optimize/adpf/thermal)), and reads missed frame deadlines from JankStats. It steps down at once and steps up only after about a minute of clean frames and thermal headroom, following ADPF's advice to "find a sustainable quality level". It promotes to Cinematic on measured evidence rather than a device list, because the research found no reliable device-class signal, while missed deadlines and headroom are direct measurements.

| Tier | Chosen when | Render scales | Update rates | Content |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Still** (new) | Remove animations or in-app Reduce motion; Auto in Battery Saver; thermal status SEVERE or worse | Sky 0.5×, drawn once per state | No ambient redraw; state changes dissolve over about 0.5 s | Lookup-table sky, clouds, horizon, a frozen pane frame; no particles, flashes, parallax or ripples |
| **Battery** | Headroom above 0.95 (about MODERATE, today's trigger); sustained missed frames; user choice | Sky 0.33×; pane off | Clouds 10–15 Hz; precipitation 30 Hz | One textured cloud deck, two precipitation layers, global flash only |
| **Balanced** (default) | Normal conditions | Composite 0.5×; clouds and fog 0.25×; pane 0.75× | Still skies 15–30 Hz; nearest streaks 60 Hz; pane 30 Hz | Two lit decks, three precipitation layers with splashes, pane, CPU-drawn bolt, gravity tilt |
| **Cinematic** | Auto, after about 60 s of clean frames with headroom below 0.7 and no Battery Saver; or user choice | Composite 0.75×; clouds 0.5×; pane 1.0× | As Balanced; High frame-rate vote only during touch and transitions | Three to four decks, god rays, lens-flare ghosts, optional aurora, rotation-vector parallax, HDR highlights once proven |

Explicit user choices stand, with two exceptions: Battery Saver caps them at Battery, and Remove animations or Reduce motion always wins.

### Budgets hold the sky to a quarter of each vsync and to today's energy

One rule governs frame time. The sky pipeline (atmosphere, clouds, precipitation, pane and final composite) may use at most a quarter of the current vsync period. That leaves the rest of the frame for glass refraction (which re-samples the sky whenever it changes), the interface and thermal headroom.

| Refresh rate | Frame budget | Sky pipeline budget |
|---|---|---|
| 60 Hz | 16 ms | **about 4.2 ms** |
| 90 Hz | 11 ms | **about 2.8 ms** |
| 120 Hz | 8 ms | **about 2.1 ms** |

The frame budgets are Android's own 16, 11 and 8 ms figures ([Android](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/vitals/render)). The quarter is a design choice, consistent with Hillaire's roughly 1 ms whole sky on an iPhone 6s and Guerrilla's 2 ms cloud target. The governor enforces the rule by stepping down when it fails. The restructuring is what makes it achievable, as this comparison for the default tier shows.

| Layer | Today, Balanced | Proposed Balanced | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sky gradient, glow, sun | Computed per pixel at 0.5×, 30 Hz | One lookup-table read plus an analytic sun at 0.5×; the table is rebuilt on the CPU when the sun moves | Cheaper per pixel, physically lit |
| Clouds and fog (by count of the shader, about 23 noise evaluations per pixel at worst) | Inside the sky shader at 0.5×, 30 Hz | Separate offscreen layers at 0.25×, 15 Hz | About 8× fewer expensive evaluations |
| Rain and snow streaks | 0.75×, 30 Hz, inside the refracted pane | Far layers unchanged; the nearest layer in its own 60 Hz pass over the rendered pane, at reduced visibility, as AOSP draws near rain | Double rate on the cheapest layer; no strobing |
| Window pane | 0.75×, 30 Hz; three drop-field evaluations plus up to three 9-sample blurs per pixel | 0.75×, 30 Hz; exact drop normals; blur taken from the cached low-resolution layers once prototyped | Drop cost up to about 3× lower |
| Idle clear sky | Re-rendered 30 times a second, with grain reseeded each frame and every glass surface re-refracting | Redrawn at 10–15 Hz or less, or not at all when drift is under a pixel; static dither | Largest saving, on the most common scene |

Frame-rate votes turn those rates into display power savings. Some devices support adaptive refresh rate (Android 15 QPR1 and later, where the display hardware allows it). On those devices the highest frame-rate vote on screen wins, touch boosts the rate to High, and a view that redraws defaults to Normal, often 60 Hz ([Android](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/animations/adaptive-refresh-rate)). Rosa's Compose BOM already includes `Modifier.preferredFrameRate` ([Android](https://developer.android.com/reference/kotlin/androidx/compose/ui/preferredFrameRate.modifier)). The sky should vote 30 Hz for still skies, 60 Hz for precipitation and High only during transitions. Every glass surface that redraws with the sky must vote the same way, or its Normal default will hold the panel at 60 Hz. Without adaptive refresh, and on API 33–34, where the vote is probably ignored (unverified), today's frame throttle in `AmbientClock` remains the fallback; it keeps an even rhythm by skipping a fixed number of display refreshes.

Energy gets a relative budget, because no absolute baseline exists. Phase 0 measures every scene at every tier with Macrobenchmark's `PowerMetric`, which runs only on a physical Pixel 6 or newer and needs fixed brightness and a stable temperature ([Macrobenchmark](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/benchmarking/macrobenchmark-metrics)). The rebuilt pipeline is then held to no increase in energy. The new Balanced may not use more GPU-plus-display energy on any scene than today's Balanced, and the new Battery may not use more than today's Battery. Still stays within a few per cent of a static screen, and any change that makes a scene more than 10% worse blocks the merge. These are proposed targets, to be calibrated against the first baseline. In the field, Rosa must stay far below Android vitals' 1% threshold for excessive battery use ([Android vitals](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/vitals)), and it must never render while not visible. Android 17's `ProfilingManager` triggers for excessive CPU use and anomalies can catch a runaway rendering loop ([Android 17](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17/features)).

Frames get targets in both the lab and the field. In the lab, every scene and tier must show a 95th-percentile `frameOverrunMs` of zero or less (Macrobenchmark, API 31+), and the aim is for the 99th percentile to be zero or less at Balanced on the mid-range reference device ([Macrobenchmark](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/benchmarking/macrobenchmark-metrics)). In the field, the metric is the share of frames that miss their deadline per scene and tier, tagged through JankStats state ([JankStats](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/jankstats)). JankStats' default jank flag is the wrong metric, because a 12 ms frame misses a 120 Hz deadline yet counts neither as JankStats jank (twice the frame period) nor as an Android vitals slow frame (over 16 ms). Time to initial display must not get worse than the Phase 0 baseline. All new sky caches together should stay within about 16 MB, alongside the existing 12 MB weather-icon cache.

### Comfort rules apply at every tier

Accessibility settings must reach the custom renderer, not only Compose's own animations. Compose has honoured the system "Remove animations" setting for its own animations since version 1.2 ([Eevis Panula](https://eevis.codes/blog/2022-12-12/android-animations-and-reduced-motion/)), but Rosa's clock, shader time and sensors need explicit checks. Rosa reads the setting once per screen context, so it misses changes made mid-session (`DS/motion/RosaMotion.kt` L32–43), and sky taps and wipes are not gated at all. Rosa also needs its own toggles. WCAG 2.3.3 names parallax as a trigger for vestibular disorders ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)). Apple's Reduce Motion makes Weather's animations static and replaces slides and zooms with dissolves ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT202655)). Android's new "reduce blur effects" setting exposes no app API that this research could find ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/android-reduce-blur-effects-setting-3601579/)). Pixel Weather's hidden tap-to-pause at the top of its screen is a precedent for a one-tap pause on the sky ([Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/google-pixel-weather-hidden-play-pause-button-animations/)).

| Signal | Detected by | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Remove animations | `ANIMATOR_DURATION_SCALE`, watched live (today read once) | Still tier; dissolves instead of slides and zooms; no tilt, camera moves, ripples or entrance cascade; wiped mist returns by fading |
| Reduce motion (new, in-app) | Settings | Same as above, without changing system animations |
| Reduce flashing (new; on with Reduce motion) | Settings | No full-screen flash; lightning becomes a slow brightening of the clouds over several hundred milliseconds; bolt without flicker; thunder carried by haptics and sound |
| Always | WCAG 2.3.1 | At most 3 flashes in any 1 s; at most 2 full-screen pulses per strike; at least 2 s between strikes; no saturated red flash; no HDR boost on flashes |
| Contrast 0.5 or higher | `UiModeManager.getContrast()` (watched today) | Denser glass (exists), plus dimming scrims behind text and opaque backing panels |
| Reduce transparency (new, in-app) | Settings | Opaque tonal surfaces instead of refraction |
| Dangerous weather | Condition (such as ThunderstormHail), or official alerts if added | No playful haptics, sounds or ripples; the hazard shown in text, icon and colour; drama only in a view the user opens |
| Ringer on vibrate or silent | `AudioManager.getRingerMode()` | Interface sounds muted; ambient sound only after an explicit opt-in |
| Battery Saver | `PowerManager.isPowerSaveMode()`, plus a receiver registered at runtime | Still tier in Auto; no ambient haptics |

The existing tab-bar contrast test (at least 4.8:1) should extend to the large numerals and card text over every new scene. Brighter sunsets and whiteouts are exactly where a richer sky could hurt legibility.

Several steps refer to a distinctness minimum. It should sit between the pairs that fail today (0.3–3.1 ΔE) and the pairs that already read as different (11–16 ΔE, rain vs snow and fog vs snow). Start near 6 ΔE and tune it by eye. That turns "every condition looks different" into a test that can fail in CI.

## Conclusion

The research turns "cinematic" for Rosa from an effects problem into a direction problem. The upgrades with the biggest visible payoff are framing the sun with a camera instead of a box, lighting the sky physically, giving it a horizon, staging weather as events and scrubbing as a time-lapse. Once each scene has settled, they also spend less GPU per second than today's full-rate noise, because they move work into CPU-built tables, cached layers and transition logic. Done in this order, Rosa can look much richer while using less energy on its most common scenes, the reverse of the usual trade-off. And because every pane of glass is lit by the sky, each sky upgrade also improves the interface, a link between scene and interface that none of the benchmarked apps documents.

The limiting factor is evidence, not technique. Nothing in Rosa has run on a device, so every millisecond and milliwatt above is a hypothesis until the Phase 0 harness produces numbers. The pane is the app's standout effect, but it is also its most expensive effect and its one licence risk, which makes its clean-room rewrite the highest-leverage single change. The leaders also show that the premium bar includes knowing when not to animate. Apple and Google gate animation by hardware and power, and Pixel turns off its playful haptics in dangerous weather. For Rosa, the Still tier, Reduce flashing and opt-in sound are part of the commercial product, not concessions to it.
