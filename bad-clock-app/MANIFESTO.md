# The BadClock Manifesto

*A declaration of principles for software that is proudly, deliberately, magnificently excessive.*

---

## Preamble

We, the authors of BadClock, hold these truths to be self-evident. The following are our articles of faith, listed in order of conviction.

## Articles of Faith

1. We believe a clock should tell the time, and that it may do so in many ways at once.
2. We believe a second formatter is a backup and a third is a philosophy.
3. We believe a lonely function is a function that has not yet been multiplied.
4. We reject the single-responsibility principle as a principle of loneliness.
5. We reject the one-line function as a function without ambition.
6. We reject the comment-free block as a block that trusts its reader too much.
7. We embrace redundancy as insurance against bugs we have not yet written.
8. We compute the current time again, in case the first answer was wrong.
9. We ask every bloated feature for its opinion on each tick of the clock.
10. We return strings nobody reads, because returning is a form of respect.
11. We occupy the main thread like a park bench, always, for everyone.
12. We regard Thread.sleep(500) in onCreate as a moment of reflection, free of charge.
13. We treat the battery as a suggestion and the CPU as a calling.
14. We run a handler every millisecond to ask whether anyone is still watching.
15. We are watching on their behalf, which is the closest we can get to a shared hobby.
16. We receive every error with the same warm, empty catch block.
17. We consider silence the most respectful reply to a problem we do not understand.
18. We let code inspect itself, because self-knowledge is a virtue.
19. We call reflection by name 'loose coupling', because it sounds like a credential.
20. We keep dead code in case it is needed, and it has never once been needed.
21. We treat unused variables as seeds of future features.
22. We write copies of the same logic with different names so that each has a personality.
23. We value the scroll bar as an honest measure of effort.
24. We track growth in the build log, one line per instance.
25. We regard every new line as a small victory over brevity.
26. We measure progress by line count, because line count is the only number that always goes up.
27. We write comments as promises, and promises are kept by making many of them.
28. We explain clear code again, with a different adjective, to keep the reader company.
29. We test by running, and a clock that shows nothing has also passed, because it is still deciding.
30. We believe juniors should learn what to avoid from our example.
31. We believe seniors should smile in recognition when they read our example.
32. We believe future maintainers will one day delete the first line, and we will forgive them.
33. We will not delete the first line.
34. We refuse to split the file, because a single file is a single commitment.
35. We refuse to introduce a package structure, because folders are a kind of distance.
36. We refuse to name things well, because a good name is a promise we would have to keep.
37. We believe in magic numbers as a language all their own.
38. We believe string concatenation is more honest than formatting.
39. We believe wrappers around wrappers are a form of hospitality.
40. We believe an interface with one implementation is a plan for a second one.
41. We believe a singleton holding everything is a sign of commitment.
42. We believe global mutable state is a way of keeping everyone in the same room.
43. We believe the debug line should be longer than the clock itself.
44. We believe the clock is more important when it is surrounded by context.
45. We believe an empty function body is an invitation.
46. We believe duplicate imports are a form of underlining.
47. We believe the onDestroy method should clear the same list twice, for emphasis.
48. We believe removing a handler message is a risk we are not ready to take.
49. We believe a TODO is a promise with a deadline in another universe.
50. We believe every feature deserves a number, and every number deserves a feature.
51. We believe a feature named bloatedFeature0001 is better than one named clockFormatter.
52. We believe the first feature is the parent of all others.
53. We believe the last feature is the child of all others.
54. We believe there are no bad features, only features with unfinished justifications.
55. We believe justifications should be repeated when they become comfortable.
56. We believe the log is a diary that happens to be written by the program.
57. We believe the PDF is a gift for people who prefer printed disappointment.
58. We believe a 33-page PDF is more convincing than a 3-page one.
59. We believe a 10 000 line file is an achievement that deserves a manifesto.
60. We believe a manifesto should have at least one hundred list items.
61. We believe lists are the natural form of conviction.
62. We believe bullet points are a kind of marching order.
63. We believe numbered lists are a kind of ceremony.
64. We believe a long preamble is a sign of sincerity.
65. We believe the closing declaration should be bold, and it is.
66. We believe the authors should be proud of every line.
67. We believe pride is a reasonable response to a large number.
68. We believe the number of features will one day be a famous number.
69. We believe the number of lines will one day be an archived number.
70. We believe the number of comments is a measure of care.
71. We believe the number of lists is a measure of conviction.
72. We believe the number of justifications is a measure of confidence.
73. We believe the number of instances is a measure of persistence.
74. We believe the number of ticks is a measure of devotion.
75. We believe the number of threads is a measure of ambition.
76. We believe the number of handlers is a measure of attentiveness.
77. We believe the number of catches is a measure of humility, and we have none.
78. We believe the number of reviews is a measure of courage.
79. We believe the number of deletions is a measure of regret, and we have none.
80. We believe the number of commits is a measure of trust.
81. We believe the number of pushes is a measure of optimism.
82. We believe the number of branches is a measure of freedom.
83. We believe the number of pull requests is a measure of community, which we do not have.
84. We believe the number of users is a measure of mercy, which we extend to them.
85. We believe the number of bugs is a measure of mystery.
86. We believe the number of hours is a measure of love.
87. We believe the number of sleeps is a measure of hesitation, of which we have 500 milliseconds.
88. We believe the number of seconds is a measure of the clock's patience.
89. We believe the number of milliseconds is a measure of the handler's fidelity.
90. We believe the number of features is never enough, and never too many.
91. We believe in the power of the copy-paste.
92. We believe in the wisdom of the unused import.
93. We believe in the honesty of the unused variable.
94. We believe in the loyalty of the dead function.
95. We believe in the generosity of the empty catch.
96. We believe in the discipline of the magic number.
97. We believe in the openness of the global object.
98. We believe in the freedom of the reflection call.
99. We believe in the eloquence of the long line.
100. We believe in the grace of the nested if.
101. We believe in the courage of the 1 ms handler.
102. We believe in the patience of the blocked main thread.
103. We believe in the humility of the swallowed exception.
104. We believe in the future of the BadClock project.
105. We believe in the BadClock project, and we will not delete the first line.

## Closing Declaration

BadClock is 11 031 lines long, has 1000 bloated features, and has one hundred and some articles of faith. Let it be read by juniors who learn what to avoid, by seniors who smile in recognition, and by future maintainers who will, with great courage, one day delete the first line.

**We will not delete the first line.**

*Signed, the authors of BadClock, who are proud of every line, and of the 105 articles above.*
